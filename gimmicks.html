The Gimmicks Catalog

The ModzVerse database contains 17 canonical gimmicks — character archetypes that workers can be assigned to define their on-screen persona. A gimmick is more than a costume; it is a structural template that affects how the simulation calculates a worker's popularity changes, angle ratings, and storyline suitability. Each gimmick has a disposition preference (Face, Heel, or Neutral) and a set of modifiers that govern the gimmick's mechanical effects.

Understanding the gimmick catalog is essential for any booking player, because the right gimmick on the right worker can transform a midcarder into a main eventer, while the wrong gimmick can sink a promising career. The catalog below documents all 17 gimmicks with their dispositions, descriptions, and mechanical modifiers.

Disposition Legend
Face — the gimmick is best suited for heroic/babyface workers. Heel — the gimmick is best suited for villainous/heel workers. Neutral — the gimmick works for either disposition.

Modifier Reference

The gimmick modifiers are the mechanical effects that each gimmick has on a worker's simulation. The table below is a complete reference of every modifier that appears in the ModzVerse gimmick catalog, with an explanation of what each modifier actually does in the simulation.

Modifier Effect Strategic Implication
Fast Pop Gain Worker gains popularity faster than baseline when booked well. Use on workers you want to push quickly; pair with strong booking to fast-track to main event.
Slow Pop Gain Worker gains popularity more slowly than baseline. Use on workers with long-term builds; the slow burn creates more credible stars.
Fast Pop Loss Worker loses popularity faster than baseline when booked poorly or absent. Risky — workers with this modifier cannot afford to be off TV. Avoid for workers who get injured easily.
Slow Pop Loss Worker loses popularity more slowly than baseline. Safe — workers can be off TV without losing momentum. Use for veterans and part-timers.
Capped Popularity Worker's popularity has a hard ceiling; cannot exceed a certain level. Midcard-only gimmicks. Do not use on workers you want to push to main event.
Match Boost Worker's match ratings are boosted. Use on workrate workers to amplify their in-ring value.
Talking Angle Boost Worker's promo/angle ratings are boosted. Use on entertainment-focused workers; particularly valuable in Sports Entertainment promotions.
Attack Angle Boost Worker's attack/brawl angles are boosted. Use on workers booked in invasion/beatdown angles.
Video Angle Boost Worker's pre-taped video segment ratings are boosted. Use on workers with strong character work but limited in-ring ability.
Requires Entertainment Skill Gimmick only works if the worker has high entertainment skill; fails otherwise. Do not assign to workrate-only workers. Verify the worker's entertainment stat first.
Requires Wrestling Skill Gimmick only works if the worker has high wrestling skill; fails otherwise. Do not assign to character-only workers. Verify the worker's wrestling stat first.
Requires Intimidation Gimmick only works if the worker has high intimidation; fails otherwise. Reserve for large, physically imposing workers. A small worker with this gimmick will fail.
Requires Low Intimidation Gimmick only works if the worker has low intimidation; fails otherwise. Reserve for smaller, sympathetic workers. A large worker with this gimmick will feel wrong.
Next Gimmick Failure The worker's next gimmick change (after this one) has a high chance of failing. Use as a "transition gimmick" — workers should stay in this gimmick for a while, not bounce out quickly.
Added Cost The promotion pays an additional cost whenever the worker is booked. Use sparingly — workers with this modifier are expensive to keep on TV. Reserve for top heels who draw.

Strategic Notes by Promotion Style

Different promotion styles favor different gimmicks. The ModzVerse's five active styles each have a distinct "gimmick palette" — a set of gimmicks that work well with the style's booking model and a set that do not.

Sports Entertainment (Chicago Show)

Chicago Show's high entToWresRatio of 7 means the promotion's roster is built around characters, not workrate. The ideal Chicago Show gimmicks are the entertainment-driven ones: Arrogant, Cocky, Millionaire, Royalty, Musical Performer, and Unstable. These gimmicks all have Talking Angle Boost or Video Angle Boost, which amplifies the entertainment value of the promotion's high-angle product. Gimmicks like Athlete (which requires wrestling skill and provides a match boost but no angle boost) are less valuable in Chicago Show, where matches are secondary to storylines.

Lucha Libre (Lucha Bellas)

Lucha Bellas' balanced entToWresRatio of 5 means the promotion can support both workrate-driven and character-driven gimmicks. The mask tradition of lucha libre favors certain gimmicks strongly: Monster (the intimidating masked heel), Supernatural (the mystical masked worker), Evil Foreigner (the foreign ruda who challenges the Mexican technico), and Underdog (the sympathetic small masked worker who fights from below). The Wholesome Face gimmick is also valuable for the technico side of the lucha dynamic.

Traditional (Lucha MEX)

Lucha MEX's workrate-driven style and high danger rating favor gimmicks that emphasize toughness and credibility: Athlete, Badass, Classic Heel, and Wholesome Face. Gimmicks that rely on entertainment skill (Arrogant, Cocky, Millionaire, Royalty) are less valuable here, because Lucha MEX's product is built around in-ring work, not angles. The Underdog gimmick can also work well, particularly for younger workers coming up through the Lucha MEX system.

Pure Wrestling (RAIBARAZU)

RAIBARAZU's low entToWresRatio of 3 means the promotion is the most sport-like in the ModzVerse. The ideal RAIBARAZU gimmicks are the ones that enhance in-ring credibility rather than entertainment: Athlete, Badass, Monster (for the giant heel archetype), and Classic Heel. The Pure Wrestling style does not reward gimmicks that depend on entertainment skill — workers with the Arrogant, Cocky, Millionaire, or Royalty gimmicks will feel out of place in a RAIBARAZU ring. The realSportsFeel aesthetic of the promotion demands workers who look like athletes, not characters.

Kings Road (Osaka Joshi, Valkyrie Project)

The two Kings Road promotions have different gimmick palettes despite sharing a style. Osaka Joshi, with its entToWresRatio of 7, can support the entertainment-driven gimmicks: Royalty, Arrogant, Cocky, and Unstable all work well for the character-driven women's wrestling product. Valkyrie Project, with its lower entToWresRatio of 4 and realSportsFeel flag, favors the more sport-like gimmicks: Athlete, Badass, Classic Heel, and Wholesome Face. The two promotions can use the same style but interpret it through fundamentally different gimmick choices.
