Promotions of ModzVerse

The six promotions of ModzVerse are the central institutions of the world. Each has its own history, its own style, its own fanbase, and its own problems. The pages below document each in full — the canonical database facts (debut dates, prestige ratings, financial positions, regional popularity scores, style classifications, production settings, drug-testing policies) and the in-universe lore that gives those facts meaning. Promotions are listed in the canonical order assigned by the world database, which roughly corresponds to their founding order with some exceptions.

History

Founded on New Year's Day, 1950, Chicago Show is the joint-oldest promotion in the ModzVerse (alongside RAIBARAZU, which debuted five months and five days later on June 6 of the same year). The promotion was established in Chicago, Illinois — a city with deep working-class roots and a long tradition of live entertainment — and from the very beginning positioned itself as a Saturday-night institution: a weekly date with the working man, the kind of event you could take your family to after a long week at the stockyards or the factory floor.

The promotion's longevity is its defining trait. In a world where the average promotion has been around for less than fifty years, Chicago Show has been running continuously for seventy-five. That has produced two compounding effects. First, the promotion has accumulated a substantial cash reserve — $50,000 on hand at the start of 2025, more than every other promotion in the world combined. Second, and paradoxically, that longevity has not translated into cultural prestige. Chicago Show enters 2025 with a prestige score of zero, the same as every other promotion in the world except Osaka Joshi. The promotion is rich but unloved, established but not respected.

The reason for this gap is the world itself. Chicago Show is a Sports Entertainment promotion in a wrestling world dominated by Japanese Pure Wrestling, Mexican Lucha Libre, and Japanese Kings Road. The high-angles, high-drama, character-driven style that real-world American promotions built their empires on is, in the ModzVerse, an outlier. Chicago Show's entToWresRatio of 7 means that for every minute of in-ring wrestling, the promotion produces roughly seven minutes of entertainment (angles, promos, backstage segments) — a ratio that would be considered excessive in Tokyo and heretical in Osaka.

Style & Presentation

Chicago Show practices Sports Entertainment, the most angle-heavy of the five styles represented in ModzVerse. The promotion's angleToWresRatio is set to 5 (the world default), but its entToWresRatio of 7 indicates that the entertainment-to-wrestling tilt of its roster is significantly higher than the world average. In practical terms, this means Chicago Show is built around characters and storylines, not workrate. A Chicago Show card is more likely to feature a twenty-minute promo segment than a thirty-minute draw, and the fans who attend a Chicago Show event are there as much for the storyline payoffs as for the matches themselves.

The promotion's impact rating of 6 (out of 10) indicates moderately high emphasis on dramatic moments — finisher kickouts, run-ins, post-match beatdowns. The danger and violence ratings of 5 are both exactly at the world default, meaning Chicago Show is not a hardcore or deathmatch promotion but is willing to use weapons and highspots when the storyline demands it. The promotion has a vocalCrowd flag set, meaning that audience reactions are amplified in the simulation — a Chicago Show crowd is loud, opinionated, and will let a worker know when they have under-delivered.

Chicago Show also has the storylinesExpected flag set, which means the simulation will penalize the promotion if it books shows without ongoing storyline threads. This is a Sports Entertainment hallmark: the promotion cannot simply book "good matches" and expect to be rewarded; it must tell stories that connect one show to the next.

Notable Events

Chicago Show runs a single weekly event, also called Chicago Show, every Sunday evening. The event is configured as Weekly recurrence with a 60-minute runtime and "Huge" importance — the highest tier in the simulation's importance scale. This makes Chicago Show one of only two promotions in the world running a weekly event (the other being Lucha MEX, though MEX does not currently have an event configured).

The promotion does not currently host any annual or touring events. This is unusual for a promotion of Chicago Show's age and financial position, and represents a significant gap in the world's structural design. A promotion with $50,000 in the bank and 75 years of history should, by all rights, have at least one signature supershow. The absence is one of the open questions a booking player will have to answer.

Strategic Position

Chicago Show enters 2025 in a paradoxical position: financially dominant, culturally marginal. The promotion has more cash than every other promotion combined, but it has zero prestige, zero regional popularity outside North America (where it sits at a modest 40), and no allies. It is the only American promotion in a world where the action is happening in Tokyo and Mexico City.

The strategic question for any player booking Chicago Show is whether to double down on the Sports Entertainment identity that has kept the promotion alive for 75 years, or to pivot toward a more workrate-driven style that could compete with the Japanese and Mexican promotions on their own terms. The first path is safe but may perpetuate the prestige gap; the second is risky and could alienate the existing fanbase, but could also unlock the international markets that Chicago Show currently cannot reach.

The promotion's most valuable asset is not its money or its history — it is its isolation. Chicago Show is the only promotion that can credibly book shows in North America without direct competition. If the player chooses to expand into Canada (where regional popularity scores for wrestling reach as high as 90 in Ontario) or the Caribbean, there is no incumbent promotion to fight. The continental vacuum is Chicago Show's to claim, if it can find the workers and the will to claim it.

Database Trivia
Chicago Show is the only promotion in the world with a non-zero starting cash balance. The $50,000 figure is roughly ten times the starting cash of Osaka Joshi (the only other promotion with any money at all) and infinitely more than the four promotions with $0. This financial advantage is the promotion's primary leverage in any talent war, and a player who fails to use it is leaving the promotion's biggest asset on the table.

History

Lucha Bellas was founded on January 1, 1980 — the same founding date as its Mexican sibling-rival Lucha MEX, a coincidence that has fueled twenty-five years of mutual resentment. The promotion was created as a women's-exclusive platform for Lucha Libre, the high-flying, mask-driven style that is Mexico's most recognizable wrestling export. Where Lucha MEX represents the older, more conservative tradition of Mexican wrestling, Lucha Bellas was founded explicitly to push the boundaries of what women's wrestling could be in a country where it had been treated as a novelty for decades.

The promotion's name — Lucha Bellas, "the beautiful fighters" — is itself a statement. It is a deliberate play on the tension between aesthetic and athleticism that defines women's wrestling in Mexico. The promotion does not deny that its workers are celebrated for their appearance as much as their workrate; it embraces it. The result is a promotion where masks, costumes, and character work are at least as important as in-ring ability, and where a worker's gimmick can carry her further than her moveset.

Despite this, Lucha Bellas enters 2025 with a regional popularity score of zero across every continent — including North America, the continent it is based in. This is the deepest weakness in the promotion's structural position. The promotion has been running for forty-five years but has, according to the world database, no measurable fanbase anywhere on Earth. Whether this reflects a failure of historical booking, a deliberate clean-slate design choice by the modder, or simply a placeholder that will be filled in later is one of the open questions of the BETA world.

Style & Presentation

Lucha Bellas practices Lucha Libre, one of the most distinctive styles in professional wrestling. The style is defined by its emphasis on aerial offense, mask-based character identities, and a structured code of honor regarding masks (in real-world lucha libre traditions, losing one's mask in a "mask vs. mask" match is the most devastating loss a worker can suffer). The promotion's entToWresRatio of 5 is exactly at the world default, indicating a balanced roster — workers who can fly, workers who can talk, and workers who can work holds.

The promotion's impact, danger, and violence ratings are all at the world default of 5, meaning Lucha Bellas is not an extreme or hardcore product. It is a wrestling promotion that prioritizes the work itself over the spectacle around it, even as it celebrates the costumes and characters that the lucha tradition demands. The vocalCrowd flag is set, meaning a Lucha Bellas crowd is loud and engaged — a hallmark of Mexican wrestling crowds, which are among the most passionate in the world.

Notably, Lucha Bellas does not have the storylinesExpected flag set, in contrast to Chicago Show and the two Japanese men's promotions. This means the promotion is not penalized for booking shows without ongoing storylines — a Lucha Bellas card can be a pure exhibition of athletic spectacle without narrative connective tissue, and the simulation will reward it. This is consistent with the lucha libre tradition, where matches are often self-contained athletic contests rather than chapters in an ongoing story.

Notable Events

Lucha Bellas hosts one annual signature event: Luchastica, scheduled for January Week 5 every year. The event is positioned as the largest women's wrestling event in the Western Hemisphere — a 90-minute supershow with "Huge" importance, hosted in North America (preferred country: Mexico, countryID 55). Luchastica is the single biggest date on the Lucha Bellas calendar and the most prominent platform for women's wrestling in the ModzVerse.

The choice of January Week 5 for Luchastica is significant. It places the event at the very start of the ModzVerse canon calendar — the first major event of the year — and pits it directly against RAIBARAZU's Battle of Rivals supershow, which is also scheduled for January Week 5. A fan in Tokyo cannot attend both; a fan in Mexico City cannot attend both. The two supershows are fighting for the same calendar slot, and the simulation will track which one draws better. This is the first flashpoint of the Lucha Civil War.

Strategic Position

Lucha Bellas enters 2025 as the most strategically vulnerable promotion in the ModzVerse. It has zero money, zero prestige, zero regional popularity across every continent, and zero allies. It is one of two promotions competing for the same Mexican fanbase, and its direct rival — Lucha MEX — is also at zero across the board, meaning the Mexican wrestling market is, in the database's eyes, entirely up for grabs. The first promotion to build a measurable fanbase will have a structural advantage that may be difficult to overcome.

The promotion's primary asset is its identity. Lucha Bellas is the only women's-exclusive lucha libre promotion in the world, and the only promotion with the cultural credibility to host a Luchastica-scale event. If the promotion can convert that identity into regional popularity — particularly in North America (where Mexican wrestling has deep roots) and South America (where lucha libre has historical influence) — it can establish a foothold that Lucha MEX cannot easily dislodge.

The Mask Question
The single most important booking decision a Lucha Bellas player will make is the mask. In lucha libre, a worker's mask is their identity, their gimmick, and their merchandise — and the moment they lose it (in a "hair vs. mask" or "mask vs. mask" match) is the most dramatic moment of their career. The ModzVerse supports this through the isMasked flag on workers. A Lucha Bellas roster without at least one or two masked workers with significant storyline investment in their masks is not really a lucha libre roster at all.

History

Lucha MEX shares its founding date — January 1, 1980 — with Lucha Bellas, a coincidence that the world's kayfabe history treats as no coincidence at all. The two promotions were born from the same split: a disagreement over the future of Mexican wrestling that pitted the old-school traditionalists against the new-wave mask-and-flash crowd. Lucha MEX is the traditionalist side of that split — the promotion that refused to embrace the mask-as-character tradition and insisted that Mexican wrestling should be about workrate, storytelling in the ring, and the older territorial style that predates the lucha libre explosion.

The promotion's name — Lucha MEX — is a direct assertion of national identity. Where Lucha Bellas chose a name about beauty and the workers themselves, Lucha MEX chose a name about the country. The promotion positions itself as the Mexican wrestling promotion, the keeper of the tradition, the place where wrestling is wrestling. The result is a promotion that is philosophically opposed to its sibling-rival on every axis: mixed-gender vs. women-exclusive, Traditional vs. Lucha Libre, workrate-driven vs. character-driven.

Despite the philosophical opposition, Lucha MEX enters 2025 with a regional popularity profile that is, in some ways, more respectable than Lucha Bellas'. The promotion has a North American popularity of 40 — matching Chicago Show — suggesting that despite its Mexican base, it has built a meaningful fanbase across the broader North American continent. Why this popularity score exists for Lucha MEX but not for Lucha Bellas is one of the mysteries of the ModzVerse database; the most likely explanation is that Lucha MEX's mixed-gender roster has historically drawn a broader audience than Lucha Bellas' women-exclusive product.

Style & Presentation

Lucha MEX practices Traditional wrestling — the catch-all style for promotions that do not commit to any of the more specialized styles. Traditional wrestling is, in the ModzVerse, the style of old-school territorial wrestling: chain wrestling, mat work, psychology-driven matches, minimal flash. The promotion's entToWresRatio of 6 is slightly above the world default, indicating a roster that can carry angles when needed but is primarily built for in-ring work.

What makes Lucha MEX distinct is its danger rating of 8 — the highest danger rating of any promotion in the ModzVerse. This means Lucha MEX workers are pushed harder, take more risks, and are more likely to be injured in the simulation than workers at any other promotion. This is consistent with the traditional Mexican wrestling style, which historically has been more physically demanding than the theatrical lucha libre style. The promotion's impact rating of 7 is also above average, indicating a hard-hitting product.

The promotion has both the vocalCrowd and storylinesExpected flags set. The storylines expectation is notable: Lucha MEX, despite being a Traditional promotion, is expected to book ongoing storylines — not just standalone matches. This places it closer to Chicago Show in structural expectations than to its sibling-rival Lucha Bellas, which is not penalized for booking storyline-free cards. A Lucha MEX player will need to think about ongoing narrative threads in a way that a Lucha Bellas player will not.

Notable Events

Unlike its sibling-rival Lucha Bellas, Lucha MEX does not currently host any signature events in the world database. This is a significant gap. A promotion with a 40-popularity fanbase in North America and a 45-year history should, by all rights, have at least one annual supershow. The absence suggests either that the modder has not yet populated the events table for Lucha MEX (a BETA status issue), or that Lucha MEX's booking model is built around regular weekly shows rather than signature events — a deliberate differentiation from Lucha Bellas' annual-supershow model.

A player booking Lucha MEX will likely need to create at least one signature event early in the canon year to give the promotion a focal point for storyline payoffs and to compete with Luchastica's positioning as the biggest Mexican wrestling event of the year.

Strategic Position

Lucha MEX enters 2025 in a stronger structural position than its sibling-rival, despite having the same $0 in the bank and 0 prestige. The promotion's 40-point North American popularity is a meaningful asset — it is the second-highest regional popularity score for any promotion in the world (behind only RAIBARAZU's 60 in Asia). This gives Lucha MEX a real fanbase to build on, where Lucha Bellas is starting from absolute zero.

The promotion's primary strategic challenge is its identity. Lucha MEX is the "old school" Mexican wrestling promotion in a world that has largely moved past old-school Mexican wrestling. The danger=8 rating means the promotion's workers will be pushed to their physical limits, which is a double-edged sword: it produces compelling matches but generates injuries. A Lucha MEX player will need to manage worker health carefully, balancing the desire for hard-hitting matches against the long-term cost of injuries.

The promotion's second strategic challenge is its lack of signature events. Without a focal point on the calendar, Lucha MEX has nothing to build storylines toward — and the simulation will penalize the promotion for booking shows without ongoing storylines (the storylinesExpected flag is set). The first Lucha MEX player will need to invent a supershow, give it a name, position it on the calendar, and start building toward it from week one.

Database Trivia
Lucha MEX is the only promotion in the world with a danger rating above 7. The 8/10 rating makes it the most physically demanding promotion in the ModzVerse — a worker signed to Lucha MEX is more likely to suffer injuries than a worker signed to any other promotion. This is a double-edged sword: it makes Lucha MEX matches more dramatic and the workers who survive them more credible, but it also means a Lucha MEX roster needs more depth (to cover for injured workers) than a typical promotion.

History

RAIBARAZU — the Japanese transliteration of the English word rivals — was founded on June 6, 1950, just over five months after Chicago Show. The promotion was established in Tokyo, Japan, at the very beginning of the post-war Japanese wrestling boom, and was conceived from the start as a promotion built on internal competition. The name is the manifesto: Rivals. The company's founding premise is that the best wrestling is produced when workers are pushed to compete not just against opponents in the ring, but against their own stablemates for roster position, brand assignment, and top billing.

This premise was operationalized in the 1980s when RAIBARAZU formally split its roster into two competing brands: Doragon (Japanese for "Dragon") as the A-brand, and Fenikkusu (Japanese for "Phoenix") as the B-brand. The Doragon roster is the promotion's flagship — bigger shows, higher-profile matches, the workers the company wants to push as stars. The Fenikkusu roster is the developmental and undercard brand — younger workers, harder matches, smaller venues, and the constant pressure to earn a call-up to Doragon. The result is a promotion that is constantly producing intra-roster drama, as workers on each brand jockey for position and the company books inter-brand dream matches on its annual supershows.

RAIBARAZU enters 2025 as the largest promotion in the ModzVerse by every meaningful metric except money and prestige. It has the highest regional popularity score of any promotion (Asia: 60, dwarfing the next-highest score of 40 held by Chicago Show and Lucha MEX). It is the only promotion with two brands. It is the only promotion with the touringCompany flag set, meaning it is configured to run touring circuits in addition to its standalone shows. It is the only promotion with a drug testing policy in effect (testing for steroids, though not for soft or hard drugs). And it is the only promotion that runs three annual supershows — Battle of Rivals, Autumn Blood Moon, and Fall of Honor — each fed by a dedicated touring cycle.

Style & Presentation

RAIBARAZU practices Pure Wrestling, the most sport-like of the ModzVerse's five active styles. Pure Wrestling is characterized by low angles (the promotion's entToWresRatio of 3 is the lowest in the world, meaning the roster is built for workrate, not for talking), high in-ring work, and a focus on athletic legitimacy. A RAIBARAZU show is, first and foremost, a series of wrestling matches — the angles, promos, and backstage segments that dominate a Chicago Show card are minimal here.

The promotion's impact rating of 7 indicates a hard-hitting product — strikes, suplexes, and submission grappling are central to the RAIBARAZU style, more so than the high-flying aerial offense of lucha libre or the character-driven brawling of Sports Entertainment. The danger rating of 5 is at the world default, meaning workers are not pushed to extreme physical risk (unlike Lucha MEX's danger=8), but the high impact rating means the in-ring work is still physically demanding.

RAIBARAZU has both the politics and storylinesExpected flags set. The politics flag means that backstage political maneuvering is part of the simulation — workers will form factions, lobby for pushes, and resent colleagues who they perceive as getting preferential treatment. Combined with the two-brand structure, this means a RAIBARAZU locker room is the most politically complex environment in the ModzVerse. The storylinesExpected flag means the player cannot simply book standalone matches; the simulation expects ongoing narrative threads, particularly around the brand rivalry.

The Two-Brand System: Doragon vs. Fenikkusu

RAIBARAZU is the only promotion in the ModzVerse with a brand split. The two brands — Doragon (A-brand) and Fenikkusu (B-brand) — are configured as separate roster units with separate importance levels (Doragon is the "A-Brand", Fenikkusu is the "B-Brand"), separate announce teams (currently empty, awaiting roster population), and separate logo assets (both currently using the placeholder logos.png file).

The Doragon-Fenikkusu dynamic is the engine of RAIBARAZU's booking. Workers on Doragon are pushed as the promotion's stars — bigger matches, higher-profile storylines, top billing on the three annual supershows. Workers on Fenikkusu are positioned as the underdogs — younger workers fighting for a call-up, veterans working stiff matches to prove they still belong, and the constant pressure of being one bad month away from being cut. The result is a promotion where every match has stakes beyond the match itself: a Doragon worker who loses to a Fenikkusu worker is being embarrassed; a Fenikkusu worker who beats a Doragon worker is making a case for promotion.

Inter-brand matches are reserved for the three annual supershows — Battle of Rivals (January), Autumn Blood Moon (September), and Fall of Honor (October). These are the only events where a Doragon worker will face a Fenikkusu worker, and they are the moments that define the promotion's annual narrative. A player booking RAIBARAZU will spend the entire year building toward these three shows, with each "Road to…" tour serving as the qualifying circuit that determines which workers earn the inter-brand spotlight.

The Tour System: Three Roads, Three Supershows

RAIBARAZU is the only promotion with the touringCompany flag set, and the only promotion that runs touring cycles. The promotion's calendar is structured around three annual supershows, each fed by a dedicated "Road to…" tour that runs in the weeks leading up to the supershow:

  • Road to Autumn Blood Moon — Tour, September, feeds Autumn Blood Moon (Annual, September Week 5)
  • Road to Fall of Honor — Tour, October, feeds Fall of Honor (Annual, October Week 5)
  • Road to Frozen Combat — Tour, February, feeds Frozen Combat (Annual, February Week 5)

Additionally, RAIBARAZU runs a standalone supershow — Battle of Rivals — in January Week 5, which is positioned as the promotion's equivalent of a "WrestleMania" (the biggest event of the year) but does not have a dedicated tour feeding it. Drug Policy & Locker Room Culture

RAIBARAZU is the only promotion in the ModzVerse with a drug testing policy. The promotion's politics flag, combined with the two-brand structure and the high-stakes annual supershow calendar, makes RAIBARAZU the most politically complex locker room in the ModzVerse. A player booking RAIBARAZU will need to manage not just the in-ring product but the backstage political dynamics — keeping Doragon stars happy with their pushes, giving Fenikkusu workers enough opportunities to feel they have a path upward, and navigating the inevitable resentment when a Fenikkusu worker is called up to Doragon over a more senior colleague.

Strategic Position

RAIBARAZU enters 2025 as the structurally dominant promotion in the ModzVerse, with one critical weakness: it has $0 in the bank. The promotion's 60-point Asian popularity is the highest regional popularity score in the world, and its three-supershow calendar is the most ambitious booking schedule of any promotion. But the lack of starting capital means the promotion cannot afford to make mistakes — a poorly-booked tour or an injured star could create a financial crisis that the promotion has no cushion to absorb.

The promotion's primary strategic asset is the two-brand system. No other promotion in the ModzVerse has the structural capacity to produce two distinct rosters of stars, and the Doragon-Fenikkusu dynamic gives RAIBARAZU a depth of storyline material that no other promotion can match. A player booking RAIBARAZU has, in effect, two rosters to manage, two sets of storylines to weave, and three annual supershows to build toward — more booking surface area than any other promotion in the world.

The promotion's primary strategic vulnerability is its complete lack of international presence. RAIBARAZU has zero popularity outside Asia. Its touringCompany flag suggests the promotion is configured to run international tours, but the current event configuration shows all tours preferred for Asia / Japan (countryID 137). The first RAIBARAZU player who chooses to take a tour outside Japan — to North America, where Pure Wrestling has a cult following, or to Europe, where the style is virtually unknown — could open a market that the rest of the ModzVerse's promotions cannot easily contest.

History

Osaka Joshi is the youngest promotion in the ModzVerse, founded on January 1, 2003 — a full 23 years after its main rival, the Valkyrie Project. The promotion was established in Osaka, Japan's third-largest city and historically a working-class, merchant-culture counterweight to Tokyo's aristocratic traditions. The choice of Osaka over Tokyo was deliberate: Osaka Joshi was founded explicitly as an alternative to the Tokyo-centered joshi puroresu scene, with a mission to bring women's wrestling to a region that had been underserved by the existing promotions.

In the 22 years since its founding, Osaka Joshi has accomplished something no other promotion in the ModzVerse has managed: it has earned measurable prestige. The promotion enters 2025 with a prestige score of 38 — the only non-zero prestige score in the entire world. This is a remarkable achievement for a promotion that is younger than every other promotion in the ModzVerse, and it positions Osaka Joshi as the most respected promotion in the world by the only metric the simulation tracks for cultural credibility.

The promotion has also managed to accumulate $2,983 in cash, making it the only Japanese promotion with any money at all (RAIBARAZU and Valkyrie Project both enter 2025 with $0). This is a small fortune by ModzVerse standards — only Chicago Show has more — and it gives Osaka Joshi a financial flexibility that its Japanese rivals lack. The promotion can afford to sign a free agent, run a non-touring standalone event, or absorb a bad month without facing financial ruin.

Style & Presentation

Osaka Joshi practices Kings Road wrestling, a style historically associated with All Japan Pro Wrestling in its 1990s peak. Kings Road is characterized by hard-hitting strikes, submission grappling, and a focus on athletic legitimacy — similar to Pure Wrestling but with a slightly higher tolerance for character work. The promotion's entToWresRatio of 7 is, surprisingly, the same as Chicago Show's, indicating that despite the Kings Road style classification, Osaka Joshi's roster is built around entertainment as much as workrate. This is a deliberate stylistic choice: the promotion is trying to bridge the gap between joshi puroresu's traditional workrate focus and a more modern, character-driven presentation.

The promotion's impact rating of 6 is above the world default, indicating a hard-hitting product. The danger and violence ratings of 5 are at the world default, meaning Osaka Joshi is not an extreme or hardcore promotion — the danger is in the strikes and suplexes, not in weapons or stunt bumps. The promotion has both the politics and storylinesExpected flags set, indicating a politically complex locker room and a booking expectation of ongoing narrative threads.

Osaka Joshi has the touringCompany flag set, indicating the promotion is configured to run touring circuits. However, unlike RAIBARAZU, Osaka Joshi does not currently have any tour events configured in the world database. This suggests either that touring is part of the promotion's intended future (the flag is set but no tours have been scheduled yet, a BETA status issue), or that the touring capability is intended for occasional special tours rather than a year-round touring structure.

Strategic Position

Osaka Joshi enters 2025 in the strongest structural position of any promotion in the ModzVerse. It has the only non-zero prestige score in the world. It has money in the bank. The promotion's primary strategic challenge is its lack of signature events. The first Osaka Joshi player will need to invent a supershow, give it a name that resonates with the Osaka working-class identity, and position it on the calendar as a counter-programming option to Valkyrie Project's three autobooked specials.

The promotion's secondary strategic challenge is its rivalry with Valkyrie Project. Osaka Joshi has the prestige, the money, and the momentum. Valkyrie Project has the AEGIS LEAGUE championship and the history. The first time these two promotions share a card — and they will, because there is no one else for either of them to fight — the entire Japanese women's wrestling scene will have to pick a side. Osaka Joshi is positioned to win that fight, but only if the player builds the roster carefully and books the rivalry deliberately.

History

The Valkyrie Project was founded on January 1, 1980 — the same founding date as both Lucha Bellas and Lucha MEX — in Saga, a prefecture on the southern Japanese island of Kyushu. The choice of Saga, a small and rural prefecture far from the Tokyo-Osaka wrestling axis, was a deliberate statement: the Valkyrie Project was founded to bring women's wrestling to regions of Japan that the Tokyo-centered joshi scene had ignored. The name — "Valkyrie" — was chosen to evoke the Norse mythological figures who chose which warriors would die in battle and which would live, a fitting metaphor for a promotion that has, over its 45-year history, made many choices about which workers would ascend to the top of the card and which would fade into obscurity.

The Valkyrie Project enters 2025 as the smallest promotion in the ModzVerse by every meaningful metric. It has zero money, zero prestige, and an Asian popularity of 12 — the lowest regional popularity score of any promotion with a non-zero rating. It has no touring company flag, no brands, and no announce team. Its only asset is the AEGIS LEAGUE championship, the only title in the entire ModzVerse, which has been defended at the promotion's three annual autobooked specials since the title's creation on January 1, 1980.

But "smallest" is not the same as "weakest." The Valkyrie Project has something no other promotion in the world has: a championship. The AEGIS LEAGUE title is the only belt in the ModzVerse, and whoever holds it is, by definition, the only champion in the world. This gives the Valkyrie Project a structural leverage that its size would not otherwise warrant. A worker who wants to be a champion has nowhere else to go.

Style & Presentation

The Valkyrie Project practices Kings Road wrestling — the same style as Osaka Joshi. But the two promotions interpret the style differently. Where Osaka Joshi has an entToWresRatio of 7 (high angles, character-driven), the Valkyrie Project has an entToWresRatio of 4 (low angles, sport-focused). This means the Valkyrie Project's product is closer to Pure Wrestling than to the entertainment-driven Kings Road of Osaka Joshi — a stylistic difference that mirrors the difference between RAIBARAZU and Chicago Show.

The promotion is the only one in the ModzVerse with the realSportsFeel flag set. This flag, in the simulation, means the promotion's product is presented as a legitimate athletic competition rather than sports entertainment — angles and character work are minimized, and the focus is on the in-ring action. Combined with the low entToWresRatio of 4, this makes the Valkyrie Project the most sport-like women's wrestling promotion in the world, and the most sport-like promotion in Japan outside of RAIBARAZU.

The promotion's impact rating of 7 is high (hard-hitting strikes and suplexes), the danger rating of 6 is above the world default (workers pushed moderately hard), and the violence rating of 5 is at the world default. The promotion has the storylinesExpected flag set, meaning the simulation will penalize the player for booking shows without ongoing narrative threads — despite the realSportsFeel flag, the player cannot simply book standalone matches.

The AEGIS LEAGUE Championship

The AEGIS LEAGUE is the only championship in the ModzVerse. Created on January 1, 1980 — the same date as the Valkyrie Project's founding — the title has a prestige rating of 50 (out of 100), making it the most prestigious championship in the world by default (it is also the only championship in the world). The title is configured as a women's singles championship (genderLimits=Women), with no weight limit, and it is currently vacant — there is no reigning champion as of January 1, 2025.

The title's configuration is unusual: defendable=0 means the title is not subject to the standard "must defend within X days" rule that most championships use. Instead, the title is defended only at the promotion's three annual autobooked specials — Valhalla (January Week 1), Twilight of The GODS (July Week 5), and The AEGIS LEAGUE Finals (January Week 5). This makes the title's lineage unusually deliberate: only 3 defenses per year, only at supershows, and the champion's reign is measured in defenses rather than days.

The AEGIS LEAGUE Finals (January Week 5) is positioned as the year's culminating event — the moment when the championship is contested at the highest stakes. The choice of January Week 5 places it in direct calendar competition with both Luchastica (Lucha Bellas) and Battle of Rivals (RAIBARAZU), making January Week 5 the single most crowded date on the ModzVerse calendar. Three promotions, three supershows, all competing for the same fan attention on the same weekend.

The Three Autobooked Specials

The Valkyrie Project runs three signature events per year, all configured with the autobook flag set, meaning the simulation will automatically book these events without requiring player intervention. This is unusual — most promotions require the player to book every show manually — and it reflects the promotion's smaller, more focused booking model. The three events are:

  • Valhalla — Annual, January Week 1, 90-minute runtime, "Huge" importance. The first event of the ModzVerse canon calendar. Preferred venue: Asia / Japan.
  • Twilight of The GODS — Annual, July Week 5, 90-minute runtime, "Huge" importance. The mid-year supershow. Preferred venue: Asia / Japan.
  • The AEGIS LEAGUE Finals — Annual, January Week 5, 30-minute runtime, "Huge" importance. The shortest of the three events — a tightly-focused championship card. Preferred venue: Asia / Japan.

The naming convention is consistent: all three events draw from Norse mythology (Valhalla is the hall of slain warriors; Twilight of the Gods is the Norse apocalypse; the AEGIS LEAGUE itself is named after the shield of Athena, blending Greek and Norse imagery). The three events form a coherent annual narrative: Valhalla opens the year, Twilight of The GODS is the mid-year climax, and The AEGIS LEAGUE Finals close the year. A player booking the Valkyrie Project has, in effect, a pre-built annual storyline structure — they just need to fill in the wrestlers.

Strategic Position

The Valkyrie Project enters 2025 in the most paradoxical strategic position of any promotion in the ModzVerse. It is the smallest promotion by every metric except one: it holds the only championship. This creates a structural asymmetry that the player can exploit in two ways.

First, the championship is a magnet for talent. Workers who want to be champions — and there is no other championship in the world to compete for — have nowhere else to go. A player booking the Valkyrie Project can use the AEGIS LEAGUE title as a recruiting tool, luring workers from larger promotions (Osaka Joshi, in particular) with the promise of a title shot that no other promotion can offer.

Second, the championship is a magnet for inter-promotional conflict. The first time Osaka Joshi's ace challenges for the AEGIS LEAGUE title, the entire Japanese women's wrestling scene will be drawn into the rivalry. The Valkyrie Project, as the title's home promotion, has the structural leverage in that rivalry — they decide who gets the shot, when, and under what conditions. A player who books the Valkyrie Project cleverly can use the championship to extract concessions from Osaka Joshi (cross-promotional shows, talent exchanges, even a merger) that the Valkyrie Project's size would not otherwise warrant.

The promotion's primary strategic vulnerability is its complete lack of money and its tiny regional popularity (Asia: 12). The Valkyrie Project cannot afford to make mistakes — a bad booking decision, an injured ace, or a failed angle could create a crisis that the promotion has no cushion to absorb. And the promotion's tiny fanbase means that even good booking decisions will produce only modest financial returns, making it difficult to build the cash reserves needed to compete with Osaka Joshi's $2,983 or Chicago Show's $50,000.

The Vacant Title Question
The AEGIS LEAGUE championship is vacant as of January 1, 2025. The first event of the canon year is Valhalla, scheduled for January Week 1 — the very first week of the year. This means the first Valkyrie Project player will, almost immediately, need to decide how the vacant title is filled: a tournament? A single match between two top contenders? An invasion angle where a worker from another promotion claims the title? The decision will set the tone for the entire canon year.
